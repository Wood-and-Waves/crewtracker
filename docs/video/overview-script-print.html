Before you record

  • Reset the demo: npm run demo:reset — all three shows, in order.
  • Open the Scheduling screen and note which weekdays the show days fall on. §3 says them out loud.
  • Sign in as dan@theaudiosmith.com, in CrewTracker Demo — not CrewTracker Shows.
  • Light mode. The paper ground is the identity and reads better compressed.
  • Phone capture ready (see over).
  • Do Not Disturb on, Mac and phone.
§4 and §5 record against Meridian Partner Summit, the finished show — it is closed out, so the punch cells are locked. To film punching live, use Northwind, which is next week and genuinely unpunched.

Recording the phone

Three beats are on a phone. Don't point a camera at it — capture the screen.

QuickTime and a cable. Plug the iPhone in. QuickTime Player → File → New Movie Recording → click the small arrow beside the record button → choose the iPhone. The phone's screen appears on the Mac, full size and sharp. You tap the real phone; the Mac records it.

This is also the only setup that can film the QR scan, because it uses the real camera.

Or iPhone Mirroring, already on this Mac — drives the phone with the mouse, but has no camera, so the QR moment won't work there.

Not the iOS Simulator. Xcode has no iOS runtime installed (a 7GB download), and it has no camera either.

Check which company the phone is signed into. dan@theaudiosmith.com belongs to both. Settings → Companies, second tab.

1 · The problem 0:00 – 0:20

The Shows list. Still, then scroll once.

If you run corporate AV shows, you already have a system. It's a group text, a spreadsheet, and somebody's handwriting on a call sheet.

This is one show in CrewTracker — from the day it's sold to the day everyone gets paid.

2 · Build the show 0:20 – 1:05

New Show. Name, venue, dates. Day activities grid — toggle Travel, Load-in, Rehearsal, Show, Load-out and let the labels appear. Add a position: 2 · Stagehand · Ballroom A · Load days. Then the PM field, choosing Ray Delgado.

A show starts with what you already know — the client, the venue, the dates. Then you say what each day actually is. Travel, load-in, rehearsal, show, load-out. Everything else is built on that.

Now you say what you need, the way you'd say it out loud: two stagehands in Ballroom A, for the load-in and the load-out. The app works out which days that is.

And you name your production manager — which sends them an invitation, not an assignment. Nothing is theirs until they accept it.

3 · Crew it 1:05 – 2:30

Send to scheduling → the queue → open the Scheduling screen. Let the grid sit a beat.
  1. Open on the Camera Operator line, first show day → pick Theo Lindqvist. No warning. Books straight in.
  2. Open on the same line, next day → Theo again, now reading Already scheduled on Cypress Dealer Meeting, button says Book anyway. Do not press it. Close the picker.
  3. Send email invites.
  4. CUT TO PHONE — the booking request. Scroll the days. Tap Accept.
  5. CUT BACK — the grid, now green.

When it's ready for crew, you send it to scheduling — and everyone who does scheduling gets it. Nobody has to work out who to hand it to.

This is their screen. Rooms down the side, days across the top, every position you need and who's in it.

Filling one is a click. Theo's free on the Wednesday, so he goes straight in.

Now the same position on Thursday. There he is again — and he's already on the Cypress job that day. It won't stop you; plenty of people work two calls. It just means you find that out now, instead of at seven in the morning when he doesn't turn up.

When the sheet is full, one press asks all of them at once. And this is what your crew actually gets.

(on the phone) Their days, what each day is, and two buttons. No login. No app to install.

(tap Accept, cut back) The sheet updates. You didn't chase anybody.

Say the real weekdays, not "the first show day" — the dates move every time the demo is reseeded. Check the grid before you start.

4 · Show day 2:30 – 3:40

PHONE. The tracker for one room. Tap Start on a crew member. Then the batch bar — Start All. Fix one time in the editor.
CUT — the printed venue sign, then a phone scanning it, picking a name, clocking in. Then tap Your hours.

Show day. This is the tracker on a phone, because that's where it gets used — in a dark room, one-handed. Tap to start. A whole room at once when the call is the same. Fix a time somebody missed.

And here's the other half. Print this sign, put it on the wall by the dock. Your crew scan it, find their name, and clock themselves in and out.

They can see their own hours, too — every day, their breaks, their overtime. Which means nobody is asking you what they worked.

5 · Payroll 3:40 – 4:35

Reports. By Day, then By Crew. The Master Summary strip. Export CSV. Send Hours on one person. Then Send Final Report and the show locking.

Then the part you actually dread.

Hours, overtime, double time, meal penalties, travel — worked out while the show was happening. By day, or by person.

Export it for whoever runs your payroll. Or text somebody their own timesheet — hours only, never rates.

And when it's done, one button sends the final report and locks the show, so nothing moves after sign-off.

6 · Close 4:35 – 5:00

Back to the Shows list.

That's one show. From the sale to the payroll, in one place — and everybody who touches it, schedulers, PMs, crew, sees the part that's theirs and nothing else.

Delivery

  • About 620 words — roughly four minutes of speech, leaving a minute of silence for the screen to do the work. Don't fill it.
  • The double-booking beat needs both halves. A warning on its own reads as nagging; the clean booking first is what makes it read as answering. It's the one moment in five minutes a spreadsheet can't do — let it breathe.
  • Three phone cuts — the booking email, the tracker, the crew clock. Half this app happens on a phone in a loading dock.
  • Nothing about settings, permissions or pricing. Those belong in the long version.
