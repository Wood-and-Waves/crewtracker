Before you record

  • Reset the demo: npm run demo:reset — all three shows, in order.
  • Open the Scheduling screen and note which weekdays the show days fall on. §3 says them out loud.
  • Sign in as dan@theaudiosmith.com, in CrewTracker Demo — not CrewTracker Shows.
  • Light mode. The paper ground is the identity and reads better compressed.
  • Phone capture ready (see over).
  • Do Not Disturb on, Mac and phone.
§4 and §5 record against Meridian Partner Summit, the finished show — it is closed out, so the punch cells are locked. To film punching live, use Northwind, which is next week and genuinely unpunched.

Recording the phone

Three beats are on a phone. Don't point a camera at it — capture the screen.

QuickTime and a cable. Plug the iPhone in. QuickTime Player → File → New Movie Recording → click the small arrow beside the record button → choose the iPhone. The phone's screen appears on the Mac, full size and sharp. You tap the real phone; the Mac records it.

This is also the only setup that can film the QR scan, because it uses the real camera.

Or iPhone Mirroring, already on this Mac — drives the phone with the mouse, but has no camera, so the QR moment won't work there.

Not the iOS Simulator. Xcode has no iOS runtime installed (a 7GB download), and it has no camera either.

Check which company the phone is signed into. dan@theaudiosmith.com belongs to both. Settings → Companies, second tab.

1 · The problem 0:00 – 0:30

The Shows list. Still, then scroll once.

If you run or staff AV shows, you most likely already have a crew tracking system. It could be a group text, slack, a spreadsheet, or someone handwriting a time sheet.

Today I want to show you a new way I've created. This is one show in CrewTracker — from the day it is sold to the day everyone gets paid.

2 · Build the show 0:30 – 1:50

New Show, in the order the words go:
  1. Name, venue, dates.
  2. The payroll preset.
  3. Day activities — Travel, Load-in, Rehearsal, Show, Load-out.
  4. The PM field.
  5. Rooms: Main Stage and Breakouts.
  6. Positions: A1, V1, LD on Main Stage · Stage Manager on show days only · 2 × Operator on Breakouts.
  7. Create show and send to scheduler.

A show starts with what you already know — The show details.

Add the show name, the venue, the dates.

You'll choose the payroll rules for this event. These payroll rules are specific to the way your organization pays their crew and are highly customizable on a per show basis.

Then you pick what the crew will be doing on those days. Travel, load-in, rehearsal, show, load-out. Everything else is built on that.

If you know who you want as Production Manager for this event, add them here and an invite email will be sent, if not, you can do this later.

Next, add the rooms you want to crew. In this case, it is Main stage and Breakouts.

Now, choose the crew roles. Main stage will get an A1, V1, and LD. We also need a stage manager for just show days, so we will add them like this. The breakout rooms need 2 operators.

Now we click create show and send to scheduler. Everyone that has the permission to schedule will get an email stating this show is ready to schedule.

The only tight beat — 73 seconds of speech in 80, a third of the video, every clause a click. Rehearse it against the real screen once.

3 · Crew it 1:50 – 2:50

  1. Cut to the mail app — the email has arrived. Click Open the schedule.
  2. The Scheduling screen for that show. Let the grid sit a beat.
  3. Open on the A1 line. Alex Reyes reads Booked on Lakeshore Investor Day — Sat 3 – Mon 5 with Book anyway. Do not press it.
  4. Click Chris Ferraro instead — books straight in.
  5. Send email invites.
  6. CUT TO PHONE — the booking request. Scroll the days. Tap Accept.
  7. CUT BACK — the grid, now green.

There is the email now. I click the schedule button and it brings me to the scheduler screen for that show.

Rooms are down the side, days across the top, every position you need and what is needed to fill.

Filling positions is one click. Alex is booked on Lakeshore Investor Day some of the days, but Chris is free, so he goes straight in.

When the sheet is full, one press sends emails to all of them at once. And this is what your crew actually gets.

(on the phone) Their days, what each day is, and two buttons. No login. No app to install.

(tap Accept, cut back) The sheet updates to show that he is booked.

If he declines, the schedulers get an email with a link back to scheduling that show.

The names are true, the dates move. Alex is on Lakeshore Sat 3 – Mon 5 and Chris is free all week — but a reseed shifts the dates, so check the picker first.

4 · Show day 2:50 – 3:50

PHONE. The tracker for one room. Tap Start on one person. Then the batch bar — Start All. Then one time set on its own in the editor.
CUT — the printed QR sign, then a phone scanning it, picking a name, clocking in. Then tap Your hours.

Show day. This is the tracker on a phone.

Tap to start. A whole room at once when the call is the same. Or individual times if they are different.

And here's the other half for the crew. Print out the QR code or send the crew their links. Your crew enters without a login, finds their name, and can clock themselves in and out.

They can see their own hours, too — every day, their breaks, their overtime. Which means nobody is asking you what they worked. The time sheet and the crew hours match.

5 · Payroll 3:50 – 4:35

Reports. By Day, then By Crew. The Master Summary strip. Export CSV. Send Hours on one person. Then Send Final Report and the show locking.

This is the reports section.

Hours, overtime, double time, meal penalties, travel — worked out while the show was happening. By day, or by person.

Export it for whoever runs your payroll. Or text a crew member their own timesheet.

And when it's done, one button sends the final report and locks the show, so nothing moves after sign-off.

6 · Close 4:35 – 5:00

Back to the Shows list.

That's one show. From the sale to the payroll, all in one place — and everybody who touches it, admin, schedulers, PMs, crew, sees the part that's theirs and nothing else.

Delivery

  • 558 words — about 3 minutes 43 seconds of speech in a five-minute video. The rest is silence for the screen to do the work. Don't fill it.
  • Only §2 is tight. Every other section has around 30 seconds of room, so the pace can be slow everywhere else.
  • Only dan@theaudiosmith.com schedules on the demo, so the §3 email arrives once rather than three times.
  • Three phone cuts — the booking email, the tracker, the crew clock. Half this app happens on a phone in a loading dock.
  • Nothing about settings, permissions or pricing. Those belong in the long version.
